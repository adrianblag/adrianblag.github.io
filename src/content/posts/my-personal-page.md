## Technical details

The idea of creating my personal page came from a college of mine. However, when I took a look into how to do it, I found that many people in my field just take some template of personal webpage such as the [github academicpages template](https://github.com/academicpages/academicpages.github.io) and add their information over it. I didn't like that idea for my personal webpage, as I found it... well, not personal. I wanted a page which I can modify as I want, but without having to pass through a steep learning curve about web development. So, what was the plan?

I really liked the modular idea of the template I linked before, so I wanted something like that for easy maintenance. In order to get rid of the web development learning phase, that I found not necessary for now, I used AI. More precisely, I used [Claude Opus 5](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-opus-5), as I found it to be very good for this use case. I fed it with all the context of the project, as well as with the indications on how I wanted the page to be. This is, I wanted the page to be modular, content based and with a simple, yet modern, look.

The workflow I followed was something like this:
1. I used [Claude Design](https://claude.com/product/design) for sketching the visual appearance of the page. I iterated on it, giving it instructions on how I wanted the page to look.
2. When I was done with the final design of the page, I switched to [Claude Code](https://claude.com/de/product/claude-code) for developing the actual page. I gave it full context of the project, as well as the final design from the previous step. This created a page initial template, pretty neat and very close to what I wanted.
3. After that, I added my specific information (publications, talks and curriculum).
4. As it is well known, AI isn't perfect and it is generally a must to iterate to achieve the final desired result. So I did. I gave *Claude Opus 5* instructions on what I wanted to change or improve until I was satisfied.

And that's for now. Regarding technical details on the tech stack used, I can not talk very much about it because of my limited knowledge on it. I can just say, as far as I know, that it used [Astro](https://astro.build/) to build the page, a web framework for content-driven websites, as it was perfect for this use case. I will probably take a look at the technical details of the page in a future as it grows, as one of the current limitations of AI right now in my opinion is the long time maintenance of a project, where humans are crucial. However, for the time being, it is a solution good enough for me.

## Artistic direction

The generic templates used for this kind of pages are, as they should be, generic. This page has some more personal artistic details that I wanted my page to have. Depending on your system default, you may have entered my page in dark or light mode. I think that it can be overlooked in light mode, but in dark mode really stands up. I am talking about color <mark>green</mark>. Well, not much to say, I really like <span class="accent">green</span>. I wanted it to be the main color of my pages, as it is part of my personality.

As for general style, I wanted to focus the page into the content itself. Not very sophisticated effects or crazy transitions, just minimalist and modern theme. It makes the page easier to maintain and to add new things, and I don't aim to develop a frontend developer level page, as that is not part of my skills.

I want the reader not to be distracted with cool artifacts or effects and rather have a pleasant and low stimuli journey in my page. In contrast, the long format content of my page can be found here, in the [Writing](https://adrianblag.github.io/writing/) section, where the more curious readers can get a deep dive in my thoughts.
